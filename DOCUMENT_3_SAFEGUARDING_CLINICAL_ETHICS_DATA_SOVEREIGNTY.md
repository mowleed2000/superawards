## SECTION 1.0: STATUTORY FOUNDATIONS, JURISDICTION & SCOPE

1.1. This Protocol establishes the operational, ethical, and clinical safety constraints governing all activities undertaken by The Superpower Project Ltd ("The Organization") within regulated adult social care environments across the United Kingdom.

1.2. The standards codified herein are mandatory, binding upon all corporate officers, deployed non-clinical facilitators, technical staff, and institutional partner organizations. This framework derives its authority from, and enforces direct compliance with, primary and secondary UK legislation, including:
* **The Care Act 2014:** Enforcing the Section 1 overarching general duty to promote individual wellbeing, Section 42 safeguarding inquiry mandates, and Section 67 independent advocacy rights.
* **The Safeguarding Vulnerable Groups Act 2006 (as amended by the Protection of Freedoms Act 2012):** Regulating regulated activity thresholds and the Independent Vetting and Barring Scheme.
* **The Mental Capacity Act 2005 (MCA 2005):** Governing capacity assessments, best-interest determination frameworks, and statutory decision-making protections under Sections 1 to 4.
* **The UK General Data Protection Regulation (UK GDPR) & Data Protection Act 2018 (DPA 2018):** Governing the lawful, fair, and transparent processing of personal data, special category health and biometric data exclusions, and data subject rights.
* **The Human Rights Act 1998:** Upholding Article 8 (Right to respect for private and family life) and Article 14 (Prohibition of discrimination).
* **The Health and Social Care Act 2008 (Regulated Activities) Regulations 2014:** Specifically Regulations 9, 10, 11, 12, 13, and 17.

## SECTION 2.0: THE "HUMAN-FIRST, TECH-INVISIBLE" OPERATIONAL MANDATE

### 2.1. Absolute Hardware & Interface Air-Gap
2.1.1. In order to eliminate the risks of disorientation, digital estrangement, or technological intimidation among vulnerable adult populations, the Organization enforces a strict physical and cognitive air-gap between the resident participant and all raw computational hardware.

2.1.2. At no point during an institutional session shall a resident participant be asked, expected, or permitted to view a digital visual interface, touch a computer or tablet screen, interact with a software application, review code outputs, or engage directly with input peripherals.

2.1.3. All hardware assets—including audio recording apparatus, computational terminals, and local edge processing units—must be managed, operated, and positioned exclusively by a trained, accredited Superpower Project Facilitator. The hardware is maintained outside the direct line of sight of the resident wherever possible, preserving an authentic, non-clinical human conversation.

### 2.2. Algorithmic Invisibility & Facilitator Mediation
2.2.1. All generative computational processes, narrative synthesis engines, and multi-sensory prompt architectures function as back-office, post-session or facilitator-mediated analytical tools. The resident experiences the session purely as a warm, human-led reminiscence and biographical capture interview.

2.2.2. Algorithmic outputs are systematically vetted, edited, and validated by human operators prior to rendering into physical, tactile heirloom formats (such as printed archival memoirs, embossed presentation albums, and tactile sensory prompt portfolios).

### 2.3. Mitigation of Algorithmic Hallucination & Cognitive Distress
2.3.1. Standard commercial generative platforms present significant clinical risks regarding confabulation, automated hallucination, and the generation of historically ungrounded narrative content. Within populations experiencing dementia, Alzheimer’s disease, or vascular cognitive decline, exposing a participant to an algorithmic hallucination regarding their personal life history can induce profound acute distress, paranoia, and lasting trauma.

2.3.2. To mitigate these risks, all narrative synthesis executed within the Superpower technical pipeline operates under deterministic, highly constrained retrieval architectures. The system is architecturally barred from inventing personal facts, dates, kinship connections, or biographical milestones. All generated text is strictly anchored to the audio-recorded testimony captured during the facilitator-mediated interview.

2.3.3. In the event that a participant expresses emotional distress, trauma, or confusion during an interview, the facilitator is mandated under Clinical Safety Standard SOP-CS-04 to immediately pause the session, terminate technical recording, and transition to empathetic grounding techniques, notifying the provider’s floor care team without delay.

### 3.1. Statutory Alignment with the Mental Capacity Act 2005 (MCA 2005)
3.1.1. In strict compliance with Section 1 of the *Mental Capacity Act 2005*, the Organization applies the five statutory core principles to every participant engagement:
1. Every adult is presumed to have capacity unless established otherwise;
2. An individual is not to be treated as unable to make a decision unless all practicable steps to help them do so have been taken without success;
3. An individual is not to be treated as lacking capacity merely because they make an unwise decision;
4. Any act done, or decision made, under this Act for or on behalf of a person who lacks capacity must be done, or made, in their best interests;
5. Any intervention must achieve its purpose through the least restrictive alternative.

3.1.2. The two-stage functional test of capacity (MCA 2005 Sections 2 and 3) is applied whenever there is documented reason to doubt an individual’s ability to consent to biographical capture, voice recording, and narrative preservation.

### 3.2. The Tripartite Tiered Consent Framework
The Organization implements a rigid, three-tier consent model to ensure absolute legal integrity across all cognitive profiles:

* **3.2.1. Tier 1: Direct Participant Statutory Consent (Full Capacity).** Where an individual has full capacity, direct written consent is secured after providing information in accessible, multimodal formats (large text, plain English, verbal explanation). The participant retains the absolute right to revoke consent, request destruction of recordings, or suspend participation at any point without prejudice.
* **3.2.2. Tier 2: Lasting Power of Attorney (LPA) Consent (Fluctuating or Absent Capacity).** Where a resident lacks capacity to evaluate the implications of biographical recording and publication, the Organization requires formal execution of the consent instrument by an authorized attorney holding a registered *Lasting Power of Attorney (LPA) for Health and Welfare* (registered with the Office of the Public Guardian) or an authorized Deputy appointed by the Court of Protection. The attorney must confirm that participation is consistent with the resident’s known lifetime values, past wishes, and personal beliefs.
* **3.2.3. Tier 3: Section 4 Best-Interests Determination Protocol (Absence of Formal Attorney).** Where a resident lacks capacity and has no appointed LPA or Court Deputy, participation can proceed only following a formal *Best-Interests Consultation* conducted in accordance with Section 4 of the MCA 2005. This consultation requires the unanimous, documented concurrence of:
  * The Care Home Registered Manager (or nominated clinical lead);
  * Identified next-of-kin, regular family visitors, or an Independent Mental Capacity Advocate (IMCA) where appointed under Section 35 of the Care Act 2014; and
  * The Superpower Project Clinical Safety Officer.  
  The determination must conclusively establish that non-pharmacological biographical capture offers identifiable therapeutic, cognitive, and psychosocial benefit to the resident, with zero identifiable psychological detriment.

## SECTION 4.0: DATA SOVEREIGNTY, ZERO-RETENTION AI ARCHITECTURE & THE EIGHT CALDICOTT PRINCIPLES

### 4.1. Integration of the Eight Caldicott Principles
The Organization treats all resident audio recordings, transcripts, photographic records, and synthesized memoirs as sensitive health-adjacent and personal biographical data. Processing protocols must comply with the Eight Caldicott Principles:

```
+----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                            THE EIGHT CALDICOTT PRINCIPLES (APPLIED)                                |
+-----------------------------------+----------------------------------------------------------------+
| 1. JUSTIFY THE PURPOSE            | Every biographical capture must serve clear therapeutic,       |
|                                   | cognitive, or identity-preservation outcomes for the resident. |
+-----------------------------------+----------------------------------------------------------------+
| 2. DO NOT USE PERSONAL DATA       | Unidentified or pseudonymous identifiers are used across all   |
|    UNLESS ABSOLUTELY NECESSARY    | computational processing; direct names are kept segregated.    |
+-----------------------------------+----------------------------------------------------------------+
| 3. USE THE MINIMUM NECESSARY DATA | Facilitators collect only the personal narratives necessary to |
|                                   | construct the agreed biographical heirloom artifact.           |
+-----------------------------------+----------------------------------------------------------------+
| 4. ACCESS ON STRICT NEED-TO-KNOW  | Only the designated facilitator and assigned data engineer can |
|                                   | access identifiable media during the active production window. |
+-----------------------------------+----------------------------------------------------------------+
| 5. AWARENESS OF RESPONSIBILITIES  | All personnel undergo continuous Caldicott Guardian and        |
|                                   | Information Governance training annually.                      |
+-----------------------------------+----------------------------------------------------------------+
| 6. UNDERSTAND AND COMPLY WITH LAW | All data pipelines fully satisfy the UK GDPR, Data Protection  |
|                                   | Act 2018, and common law duties of confidentiality.            |
+-----------------------------------+----------------------------------------------------------------+
| 7. DUTY TO SHARE IS AS IMPORTANT  | Life-story insights are shared safely with registered care     |
|    AS THE DUTY TO PROTECT         | staff to directly improve personalized resident care plans.    |
+-----------------------------------+----------------------------------------------------------------+
| 8. INFORM INFORMED EXPECTATIONS   | Transparent, accessible explanations of data handling are      |
|                                   | provided to all residents and their legal families.            |
+-----------------------------------+----------------------------------------------------------------+
```

### 4.2. Lawful Basis and Special Category Processing under UK GDPR
4.2.1. The lawful basis for processing personal data under Article 6(1) of the UK GDPR is **Article 6(1)(a) (Explicit Consent)** via the Tripartite Consent Framework, or alternatively **Article 6(1)(f) (Legitimate Interests)** where balancing tests demonstrate profound therapeutic benefit that does not override the fundamental rights of the data subject.

4.2.2. Processing of Special Category Data—specifically data revealing racial or ethnic origin, religious or philosophical beliefs, or data concerning health (including cognitive health records and mobility notes)—is strictly executed under **Article 9(2)(a) (Explicit Consent)** and **Article 9(2)(h) (Health or Social Care)**: processing necessary for the provision of health or social care or treatment, or the management of health or social care systems and services.

### 4.3. Zero-Retention Ephemeral Processing & Machine Learning Prohibitions
4.3.1. Under no circumstances shall personal biographical data, audio recordings, interview transcripts, or resident likenesses be used to train, fine-tune, or calibrate public or third-party Machine Learning (ML) models, Large Language Models (LLMs), or commercial neural networks.

4.3.2. All computational transformations (e.g., speech-to-text transcription, acoustic clean-up, narrative alignment) are executed within an encrypted, sovereign UK-based virtual environment utilizing dedicated, non-retaining API endpoints. Contractual architectures legally bind infrastructure providers to an immediate **Zero-Retention Ephemeral Processing Mandate**: data submitted to computational pipelines is processed in-memory and instantly purged upon output generation.

4.3.3. Long-term raw audio recordings are stored on AES-256 encrypted, hardware-isolated physical media during production. Upon physical delivery and sign-off of the final archival heirloom to the resident or their legal estate, all raw working media residing within cloud storage environments is systematically deleted via DoD 5220.22-M compliant electronic sanitization protocols within thirty (30) calendar days, unless an explicit written extension is executed by the resident's legal representatives.

## SECTION 5.0: VETTING, DBS ARCHITECTURE & SECTION 42 SAFEGUARDING ESCALATION

### 5.1. Personnel Vetting & The Enhanced Disclosure Barring Scheme (DBS)
5.1.1. No individual shall be deployed as a Superpower Project Facilitator or granted physical access to a partner care facility without completing rigorous pre-employment vetting, which includes:
* An **Enhanced DBS Check with Adult Barred List Checks** verified via the DBS Update Service on an ongoing basis;
* Independent verification of two satisfactory professional references;
* Verification of a continuous five-year employment, residential, and educational history;
* Mandatory completion of the **Care Certificate Safeguarding Adults (Level 2 and Level 3)** certification;
* Comprehensive training in Dementia Awareness, Managing Challenging Behavior, and Caldicott Information Governance.

### 5.2. Section 42 Safeguarding Incident Escalation Protocol
5.2.1. Facilitators are trained to identify indicators of abuse, institutional neglect, financial exploitation, physical injury, or systemic psychological distress during their narrative interactions.

5.2.2. Where an active disclosure of abuse or significant safeguarding concern is identified:
* **Step 1:** The facilitator must immediately ensure the resident’s safety without displaying alarm, judgment, or leading the conversation. The interview is suspended immediately.
* **Step 2:** The facilitator must make contemporaneous, verbatim written notes, capturing exact phrasing, timestamps, visible physical marks, and behavioral cues.
* **Step 3:** The facilitator must report the matter directly to the on-duty Registered Manager or Clinical Lead of the care home within 30 minutes, securing a signed acknowledgment of receipt.
* **Step 4:** The facilitator must notify the Superpower Project Nominated Safeguarding Lead within 60 minutes.
* **Step 5:** If there is reasonable suspicion that the provider institution itself is complicit in systemic abuse or neglect, or if the Registered Manager fails to act within two hours, the Superpower Safeguarding Lead must bypass the internal hierarchy and file a direct statutory referral to the host **Local Authority Safeguarding Adults Board (SAB)** under Section 42 of the *Care Act 2014*, and simultaneously notify the Care Quality Commission (CQC) and local police services where criminal thresholds are met.

### 5.3. Institutional Insurance & Liability Architecture
5.3.1. The Organization maintains a bespoke, high-tier institutional insurance portfolio underwriting all operational footprints:
* **Public & Products Liability:** Indemnification up to **£5,000,000** for any single occurrence;
* **Professional Indemnity & Clinical Malpractice:** Cover up to **£5,000,000**;
* **Cyber Risk & Data Breach Liability:** Dedicated institutional policy covering up to **£2,000,000**.

## SECTION 6.0: INTELLECTUAL PROPERTY, MORAL RIGHTS & BIOGRAPHICAL SOVEREIGNTY

### 6.1. Absolute Assignment of Copyright under the CDPA 1988
6.1.1. In alignment with Section 11 of the *Copyright, Designs and Patents Act 1988 (CDPA 1988)*, The Superpower Project Ltd explicitly repudiates any claim of ownership, title, or intellectual property rights over the personal narratives, memoirs, recordings, or synthesized life stories generated through its programs.

6.1.2. The Organization executes an irrevocable, perpetual, worldwide assignment of all copyright, title, and interest in the resulting tangible memoirs, life-story albums, and transcribed narratives directly to the resident participant, which upon their passing passes unencumbered to their legal heirs or designated estate executors.

### 6.2. Inviolability of Moral Rights
6.2.1. The Organization formally recognizes and upholds the moral rights of the participant under Chapter IV of the CDPA 1988:
* The right to be identified as the author of their personal narrative (Section 77);
* The right to object to derogatory treatment of their work or distorted presentation of their personal life story (Section 80);
* The absolute right not to have a life story falsely attributed to them (Section 84).

6.2.2. The Superpower Project Ltd is granted solely a non-exclusive, revocable, royalty-free operational license strictly limited to the duration required to assemble, edit, print, and bind the physical artifacts, after which its operational license terminates automatically.
